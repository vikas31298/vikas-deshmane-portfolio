Overview

Business context

Millions of Indian consumers were thin-file or new to credit. They applied to lenders blindly, were rejected, and every rejection hurt their bureau score. Lenders in turn could not assess these customers with bureau data alone.

Responsibility

My role

Owned the loan and credit-card origination product: eligibility, matching, application and hand-off to lenders. Built the ML underwriting and decisioning platform on alternative data and led integrations with lender, bureau and payment partners.

Approach

Product strategy

  1. Lead with eligibility: show customers only the offers they are likely to be approved for, instead of letting them apply blind.
  2. Use alternative data such as SMS transaction patterns and digital footprints to assess thin-file customers.
  3. Run loans and credit cards through one origination flow, with lender-specific rules plugged in per partner.
  4. Integrate lenders, bureaus and payment partners through standard APIs so new partners could be added quickly.

Technology

Architecture

Customer data and consented alternative data feed an ML underwriting layer that scores risk and income. A matching engine applies each lender's rules and routes applications to lender APIs, with bureau and payment integrations alongside.

Judgement

Key decisions

  • Showed pre-qualified offers first, trading a smaller offer list for higher approval rates and protected credit scores.
  • Used ensemble models (XGBoost, Random Forest, neural networks) because no single model was reliable on thin-file data.
  • Kept lender rules configurable per partner instead of building one global policy.

System architecture

End-to-end architecture

Customer and alternative data feed ML underwriting, a matching engine applies each lender's rules, and applications flow to lenders through standard APIs.

Request lifecycle

Request lifecycle

  1. Check: the customer pulls a free credit report and sees their score.
  2. Enrich: with consent, alternative data adds signals for thin-file customers.
  3. Score: the model ensemble estimates risk and income.
  4. Match: the engine applies each lender's and issuer's rules and shows only likely approvals.
  5. Apply: one application form is mapped to each lender's format and documents are collected once.
  6. Submit: applications go to lender APIs and statuses flow back to the customer.
  7. Learn: approval outcomes feed back into the models and matching rules.
