Business context

The problem

Dhani's super app served 10M+ users across e-commerce, healthcare and financial services. Each line of business needed order intake, decisioning, authorization and servicing, but the lending journey in particular had to scale to tens of thousands of applications a day without reliability regressions or rising contact rates.

My role

Scope and ownership

Owned product vision and roadmap for the order and transaction platform. Defined validation, orchestration, retry and intelligent-routing behaviour across the application-to-disbursal lifecycle. Led incident learnings and root-cause analysis with operations and support, and coordinated 15+ cross-functional teams.

Product strategy

How I approached it

  1. Built a single system of record for orders and transactions so each line of business composed on shared primitives instead of rebuilding them.
  2. Moved to an event-driven architecture across 12 microservices with Kafka so each lifecycle stage could scale and fail independently.
  3. Prioritised reliability work by failed-interaction and contact-rate impact, not by ticket count.
  4. Partnered with data science on XGBoost propensity models and A/B experiments to grow cross-sell on the same platform.

Tech architecture

How the platform is put together

Application intake publishes events to Kafka; validation, KYC and fraud checks, decisioning, authorization and disbursal run as independent microservices with idempotent retries and routing rules. Monitoring and alerting feed a defect-reduction loop owned jointly with operations.

Key decisions

Trade-offs I would defend

  • Chose event streaming over synchronous orchestration so a slow downstream partner could not stall intake.
  • Made retry and routing behaviour explicit product requirements rather than leaving them to implementation.
  • Ran executive trade-off reviews with finance and risk so growth experiments and credit policy stayed aligned.
