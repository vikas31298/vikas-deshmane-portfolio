Overview

Business context

Legal and procurement teams reviewed every vendor and customer contract by hand against internal policies that lived in people's heads and scattered documents. Reviews were slow, inconsistent between reviewers, and business teams waited days for simple answers.

Responsibility

My role

Owned the product from problem definition to launch: the policy playbook model, clause extraction, redlining logic, chatbot experience and the guardrails needed for legal use.

Approach

Product strategy

  1. Turn each organisation's policy into a structured playbook of clauses, positions and fallback wording.
  2. Extract and classify every clause, then compare it with the playbook rather than asking the model for open-ended opinions.
  3. Show redlines as tracked changes with a reason and a link to the policy, so reviewers stay in control.
  4. Add a chatbot so business users can ask questions about any contract without waiting for legal.

Technology

Architecture

Uploaded contracts are parsed and split into clauses. Each clause is classified, compared against the organisation's policy playbook, and redlined with suggested wording. A retrieval-backed chatbot answers questions over the same clause index.

Judgement

Key decisions

  • Compared clauses against a written playbook instead of letting the model judge contracts freely, which made outputs explainable.
  • Kept a human approval step on every redline, because legal liability stays with the reviewer.
  • Built the chatbot on the same clause index as redlining, so answers and redlines never disagree.

Agentic AI architecture

End-to-end architecture

A pipeline of specialised agents parses the contract, compares every clause with the organisation's own policy playbook, drafts redlines, and powers a chatbot over the same clause index. A human approves every change.

Request lifecycle

Request lifecycle

  1. Upload: a reviewer uploads a contract; it is parsed and split into clauses.
  2. Classify: the classifier agent labels each clause type, such as liability, termination or payment terms.
  3. Compare: the policy agent checks each clause against the organisation's playbook position.
  4. Redline: the drafting agent proposes replacement wording from the fallback library with a short reason.
  5. Summarise: the risk agent lists the highest-risk deviations for the reviewer.
  6. Approve: the reviewer accepts, edits or rejects each change and exports a tracked-change document.
  7. Ask: business users ask the chatbot questions; answers cite the exact clause.
